## SECTION 4 — THE CORE PROBLEM: MARGIN-MIX, NOT DEMAND

> **Orders exist. The problem is they're below margin floors.**

This is the critical Phase 0.5 reframe. Earlier thinking was "Cotton can't find buyers." The interview corrected this:

**Current reality**:
- Current customers are mostly **white-label / mass tier** — demanding prices that don't cover costs properly
- Mid-tier domestic brands and export buyers exist but **won't engage without compliance certs** (ISO / OEKO-TEX / SEDEX / BSCI)
- Filling to 75% at today's customer mix would still bleed cash

**The correct priority order**:
1. ~~Outbound sales first~~ → **Cert sprint first (Dilip)**
2. ~~Fill to 75%~~ → **Fill to 75% at margins above floor** (decline below-floor orders)
3. ~~Hire Cotton sales lead immediately~~ → **Hire once certs are moving** (before certs = nothing to sell into mid/export)

### Margin Hierarchy
> **Volume at good margins > Volume at bad margins > Idle capacity > Volume at loss-making margins**

**Idle is better than loss-making.** Decline below-floor orders. Protect margin.

### ⚠️ Hidden Cost Input — Linkd Inter-Company Rate (Phase 3 Finding)

LD Cotton Mills is billed by Linkd Prints at **₹35–40/mtr for 62" printing** — the standard external market rate for 62" printing is ₹28.

Cotton is paying **₹7–12/mtr above market rate** for its fabric printing input. On a garment using 1.5–2 mtrs of printed fabric, this adds ₹10–24 per garment in avoidable input cost. On a 30,000 garments/month production run, this is ₹3–7 lakh/month of above-market cost baked into Cotton's economics.

**This partially explains why Cotton's margins are below floor even with orders in hand.**

**Action (Dilip + Raghav):** Inter-company rate review must cover Linkd → Cotton pricing alongside the Silk → Linkd break-even question. A consistent group-level inter-company pricing policy must be established. Until Cotton rates are brought to market (₹28), Cotton's margin calculations are artificially depressed by an avoidable group-level accounting decision.

## SECTION 8 — SCIENCE: MANUFACTURING TIERS

### Three Tiers at Cotton

#### MASS
- Customer: domestic mass retailers, white-label for value brands
- Price point: Lowest
- Volume: High-volume, repeat of standard SKUs
- DHU target: <40–50
- Compliance: Basic — certs not required
- Fabric: Commodity cotton, poly-cotton blends
- Lead time: 30–45 days from PO

#### MID (Target Tier for Fill)
- Customer: domestic branded apparel, export-lite buyers
- Price point: 1.5–2× mass
- Volume: Medium runs with customization
- DHU target: <20–30
- Compliance: ISO 9001 preferred, OEKO-TEX useful
- Fabric: Higher-count cottons, combed yarns
- Lead time: 45–60 days from PO

#### EXPORT
- Customer: international buying houses, global brands, EU/US retailers
- Price point: 2–4× mass
- Volume: Style-specific, season-driven, tight windows
- DHU target: <5–15
- Compliance: **All 4 certs required** — ISO 9001, OEKO-TEX, SEDEX, BSCI
- Fabric: Certified, traceable, often buyer-specified
- Lead time: 60–90 days with milestone reviews

## SECTION 9 — SCIENCE: QUALITY & DEFECTS (GARMENT)

### DHU — Defects Per Hundred Units (Key Metric)

| Tier | Target DHU | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Mass | <40–50 | Acceptable for price-sensitive domestic |
| Mid | <20–30 | Branded domestic, some export |
| Export | <5–15 | International buyer requirements |
| Premium | <3 | Only for high-price goods |

**Cotton baseline target**: Lock current DHU within 30 days. Benchmark against mid-tier targets. Improve month-over-month.

### Garment Defect Categories

**Critical defects (REJECT)**:
- Holes in visible areas
- Open seams in load-bearing joints
- Missing operations (buttons, labels, zippers)
- Wrong size or wrong shade

**Major defects (Rework or Reject)**:
- Open seams in non-critical areas
- Broken stitches beyond tolerance
- Stains on visible areas
- Measurements out of tolerance
- Puckering
- Wrong thread / shade mismatch
- Poor buttonhole / bartack

**Minor defects (Pass or Minor Rework)**:
- Loose threads
- Slight puckering within tolerance
- Minor shade variation within tolerance
- Small stains on invisible areas

### SPI — Stitches Per Inch

| Fabric Weight | Typical SPI |
|---|---|
| Light shirting | 12–14 |
| Medium apparel | 10–12 |
| Heavy bottomwear | 8–10 |
| Denim | 7–9 |

Document SPI standard per product type at Cotton. Audit weekly.

### AQL Sampling (Acceptance Quality Level)

| Lot Size | Sample Size | Accept If |
|---|---|---|
| <500 units | 32 | ≤2 major defects |
| 500–3,200 | 50 | ≤3–5 major defects |
| >3,200 | 80 | ≤5–7 major defects |

Tighten for export buyers (they require stricter AQL).

### Fabric-to-Garment Flow (Cotton's Production Sequence)
1. **Raw fabric** from LD Silk Mills / Cotton suppliers
2. **Cutting room** — pattern making, cutting (cutting master)
3. **Stitching lines** — sewing per spec (stitching master + line supervisors)
4. **Finishing** — washing, pressing, final QC (finishing master)
5. **Packing + dispatch** — to buyer or Vhagar warehouse

### Quality Brain Rules
1. DHU is not optional. Cotton without a DHU baseline is flying blind.
2. Every defect category has an owner. Pattern defects → cutting master. Sewing defects → line supervisor.
3. Photograph every rejection. Build a visual library so standards don't drift with staff turnover.
4. D65 light box for shade consistency approval — non-negotiable.
5. Repeat defects are process problems, not worker problems. Fix the process.
6. Customer complaints go into the defect library. Every external complaint makes internal QC better.
