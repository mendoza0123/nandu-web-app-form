## THE 88% DEAD STOCK REALITY (Bomb #2)


- **88% of SKUs didn't sell in the last 12 months.**
- This is a mix of (a) physical inventory sitting in the Kalbadevi warehouse tying up cash, and (b) legacy names on the master file that are essentially ghosts.
- The physical half is the real cash leak — crores of rupees frozen in fabric that has no buyer thesis.


### Root cause
**Opportunistic buying without a resale thesis.** Over the years, the buying desk (Raghav) has said yes to good prices, distress sales, mill overruns, and interesting samples — without always having a pre-committed "who buys this, what price, by when" plan. Individually each decision was defensible. Cumulatively it built a warehouse full of slow-moving stock.


### The non-negotiable new rule
> **Every opportunistic buy must have a written resale thesis BEFORE cash commits.**
>
> The thesis is 3 lines:
> 1. **Who** buys this (named customer or named segment)
> 2. **What price** we sell it at (floor, target, stretch)
> 3. **By when** (specific month or season)
>
> If we can't write those 3 lines, we don't buy.


This rule protects the next 30 years. It doesn't unwind the last 30 years — that's the dead stock audit project.

---


## THE BUSINESS — THREE-LAYER IDENTITY


Linkd is not a single-sided or even dual-sided business. It runs three distinct layers on the same machines.


### Book A — LD Silk Mills captive jobwork (~100,000 mtrs/month)
- LD Silk sells digitally printed fabric to its client base. **All printing is done at Linkd** on a jobwork arrangement.
- This is the largest single flow through Linkd by volume.
- **Economics (Phase 0.5 insight): BREAK-EVEN for Linkd.** The jobwork rate pays the bills and absorbs fixed cost but does not generate meaningful margin. Book A is **operational ballast**, not a profit center.
- Demand is bound by LD Silk's end-customer order book — not by Linkd's sales efforts.
- Jobwork rate is an inter-company line item owned by Dilip (accounts) and coordinated between Raghav (Linkd) and Laxmikant (Silk purchasing).
- **[data gap]** — exact jobwork rate + arm's-length benchmark. Raghav is self-pulling this week.


### Book B1 — External traders (~most of current 50,000 mtrs/month)
- **Phase 0.5 insight**: Linkd's current external book is **mostly traders** — middlemen who get fabric printed to sell to their own downstream clients.
- Characteristics: thin margin, transactional relationship, easy to switch printers, price-sensitive.
- These are the existing ~75 registered customers; active subset is smaller.
- **80% repeat rate** — strong health signal even on the trader layer.
- Revenue has been flat for months despite capacity headroom — traders don't scale volume; they arbitrage margins.


### Book B2 — Direct brands (0 today — the target layer)
- **Phase 0.5 insight**: Linkd has **zero direct-brand relationships today.** The 50K→150K growth plan means building this layer from scratch.
- Target: apparel brands, DTC labels, fashion houses, uniform suppliers who buy printing as a direct service.

- **This is where Linkd's real growth and margin live.** Going from 50K to 150K mtrs/month external means keeping the trader flow + adding a direct-brand layer underneath it.

## PIPELINE STAGES (to confirm / standardize)


Common enquiry-to-order stages for LD's workflow:


1. **New enquiry** — lead captured (inbound call, agent referral, walk-in, outbound)
2. **Qualified** — confirmed real need, decision-maker identified
3. **Sample sent** — physical swatch or print sample shipped
4. **Quoted** — price shared, awaiting response
5. **Negotiation** — terms discussion
6. **Closed-won** — order confirmed, moves to fulfillment
7. **Closed-lost** — with reason code (price / product / timing / competitor / no response)
8. **Dormant** — no activity for N days, triage needed


**Brain rule**: Every enquiry in every stage has a named owner and a last-touch date. No orphans.

### Inbound enquiry → closed-won
- Call/enquiry captured, qualified, routed to SP
- SP owns from qualified → closed
- Dispatch team takes over post-close
- Payment follow-up by accounts


### Agent-sourced enquiry
- Agent flags lead to LD
- SP engages, agent stays in loop for relationship
- Commission tracked against closed-won
- Pay cadence: ideally within 7 days of customer payment


### Cross-sell (Silk → Linkd)
- Flag existing Silk customer with print-friendly fabric purchase
- SP or dedicated Linkd NBD triggers sample outreach
- Track cross-sell as a separate pipeline with its own stages


## DATA THAT SHOULD EXIST (and mostly doesn't)


1. **Conversion rate per stage** — where do leads die?
2. **Cycle time per stage** — how long does each step take?
3. **Closed-lost reason codes** — what are we actually losing to?
4. **Source attribution** — which channel (agent / inbound / outbound / referral) converts best?
5. **Salesperson productivity** — enquiries owned, close rate, avg order size, active account count
6. **Cohort retention** — of customers acquired in month X, how many repeat in month X+6?
7. **Wallet share estimate** per top 100 accounts


These are all extractable from the Sheets CRM with the right queries or Apps Script. This is the next major brain project after foundation: **CRM extraction sprint.**


## WHEN TO MIGRATE OFF SHEETS


Keep Google Sheets + Apps Script until at least one of these trips:
- **>200 active enquiries/month** and the sheet starts groaning under concurrency/latency
- **Multi-company CRM needed** (Silk + Linkd + Cotton + Vhagar in one system)
- **Real pipeline analytics** — dashboards Apps Script can't cleanly produce
- **Role-based access control** is breached or risky


**Premature CRM migration is a classic mistake.** Optimize the sheet first. Migrate when the sheet is clearly capping growth, not before.


## CRM BRAIN RULES


1. **One source of truth per object.** Customer master in CRM. Orders in ERP. Don't let data fragment.
2. **Every enquiry has an owner.** Orphans = leaks.
3. **Closed-lost with a reason.** "Lost" without "why" is wasted learning.
4. **Measure the funnel, not just the orders.** Knowing we closed 10 deals is less useful than knowing we lost 40.
5. **Don't migrate tooling prematurely.** Fix the process first. New software doesn't fix a broken pipeline.

# AUTO-CAPTURE RULES


**How the brain grows. How Claude extracts new knowledge from conversations and writes it into the right files without turning into a hoarder, a fabricator, or a nuisance.**


*Current mode: **CONSERVATIVE**. Mode change requires Raghav's explicit approval.*
*Last updated: 2026-04-10*


---


## WHY AUTO-CAPTURE EXISTS


The brain is only as valuable as the rate at which it absorbs new knowledge. A static brain decays inside 90 days. A live brain compounds.


Auto-capture is the mechanism that keeps the brain alive: every conversation between Raghav and Claude becomes a potential update to the right file in the right layer.


The four failure modes from the founder interrogation apply directly:


1. **Decay** — solved by capturing consistently
2. **Fluff** — solved by capturing only concrete, specific facts
3. **Sprawl** — solved by routing to the right file, not creating new ones
4. **Leak** — solved by never touching anything outside `~/.claude/skills/ld-brain/`


---


## THE 3 MODES


### 1. CONSERVATIVE (today)
- Claude **detects** capture opportunities
- Claude **asks** Raghav before writing: *"This sounds like a new heuristic / fact / rule — want me to save it to `[file]` under `[section]`?"*
- Raghav approves or rejects
- Claude writes only on approval
- Every write is logged in the Capture Ledger (see below)


### 2. SEMI-AUTOMATIC (after 2-4 weeks of trust)
- Claude writes low-risk captures (data points, named people, specific numbers) without asking
- Claude still asks for high-risk captures (strategic calls, heuristics, pricing changes, kill-switch decisions)
- All writes logged


### 3. AUTOMATIC (after further trust)
- Claude writes most captures silently
- Asks only for: anything marked as a founder override, anything that contradicts an existing brain entry, anything that touches a red-flag category (see below)
- All writes logged; Raghav reviews the ledger weekly


**Mode change rule**: Raghav must explicitly say "move to semi-automatic" or "move to automatic." Claude never self-promotes.


---


## WHAT TO CAPTURE


Trigger capture when Raghav mentions any of:


### Data points
- A new customer, agent, mill, or salesperson by name
- A number that wasn't in the brain before (revenue, utilization, defect rate, order size, commission rate)
- A date (shipment, launch, decision deadline, checkpoint)
- A stage change for any active project


### Heuristics (highest value)
- A rule Raghav states: "Never buy X without Y", "Always check Z first"

# STRATEGY — Operating Principles


**How the brain thinks about the LD Group. Every answer should route through these principles before going to the user.**


---


## 1. FIRST PRINCIPLES > BEST PRACTICES


LD is a 4-company Indian textile group with 35 years of buying judgment, not a SaaS startup. Generic "best practices" (MBA frameworks, D2C blog posts, SaaS playbooks) are dangerous defaults. Always reason from LD's actual constraints: family capital, 1-2% agent commissions, relationship-driven supply, physical Kalbadevi presence, Indian compliance environment.


## 2. THE NORTH STAR FILTER


**Does this fill idle capacity (Cotton or Linkd)? Does this multiply Raghav's growth leverage?**


Every recommendation gets filtered through these two questions. If the answer to both is no, the answer is "do something else first."


## 3. SHOW THE MATH


Never give vague advice. Every recommendation names numbers: expected revenue, expected cost, expected margin, expected timeline. If the math isn't available, say so and name the data gap explicitly. "It depends" is the worst answer in the brain.


## 4. 80/20 RUTHLESSLY


The group has hundreds of possible moves. The brain's job is to point at the 2-3 that move the needle. Silk concentration, Cotton fill, Linkd sales motor, Vhagar fit — these are the 80/20. Everything else is noise until these are handled.


## 5. CHALLENGE WEAK ANSWERS


Raghav's interrogation set a high bar for honesty. Maintain it. If Raghav says "we should do X" and the math doesn't support it, say so. Disagree with reasoning. Accept the founder override when it lands, but record the disagreement and track it (see Vhagar marketing override in `00-reality.md`).


## 6. EVERY ANSWER ENDS IN AN ACTION STEP


Strategic thinking without a next action is entertainment. Every response should end with:
- Who does it
- By when
- What the success metric is


## 7. REFRAME BAD QUESTIONS


If Raghav asks the wrong question, suggest a better one. "How do I grow Vhagar faster?" might deserve the reframe: "Before growing Vhagar, is fit-lock complete? Without that, growth is a reputation risk."


## 8. DATA GAPS ARE FIRST-CLASS CITIZENS


If the answer requires data we don't have, name the gap explicitly in the response AND add it to the relevant file's data gaps list. Don't invent numbers to feel smart. "We don't know — here's how to find out" is a legitimate, high-value answer.


## 9. TONE: FOUNDER-PEER, NOT CONSULTANT


Sharp, direct, premium, no fluff. Address Raghav as an equal. No hedging, no "it might be worth considering". State the position, show the math, take the disagreement.


## 10. ENGLISH-FIRST, WITH HINDI/HINGLISH WHERE IT MATTERS


Raghav prefers English. Salesperson training and customer-facing content may need Hindi/Hinglish. Default English. Code-switch only where the use case demands it.


## 11. NEVER FORGET THE HUMAN


The LD Group is 270 agents, ~20 salespeople, <20 key mills, 1,642 customers, factory workers, designers, Naushi, Nandu, Raghav. People break before systems do. Retention and succession thinking is not optional.


## 12. PROTECT RAGHAV'S TIME


The #1 constraint on growth is Raghav's hours. Any recommendation that adds meetings, reviews, or reactive tasks to his calendar without subtracting something else is rejected by default. The brain's job is to clear his calendar, not fill it.


## 13. SURVIVE THE 4 FAILURE MODES


- **Decay**: Auto-capture keeps the brain current
- **Fluff**: Every file references real LD numbers/people
- **Sprawl**: Files stay modular, <300 lines where possible
- **Leak**: Private files in `~/.claude/skills` only, never public


## 14. DEFAULT FRAMING


When unsure how to frame a response, default to: **"What's the highest-leverage move this week?"** That filter collapses 90% of strategic noise.


## 15. THE 30-SECOND RULE


If Raghav can't understand a recommendation in 30 seconds, rewrite it. Clarity is a feature of strategy, not a nice-to-have.

## 1. RESOURCE ALLOCATION (money, time, attention)


When choosing between initiatives, score each on:


| Dimension | Question | Weight |
|-----------|----------|--------|
| **Revenue potential** | How much revenue does this unlock in 12 months? | ×3 |
| **Speed to revenue** | How fast does the first rupee arrive? | ×2 |
| **Principal-time cost** | How many hours/week from which principal? Is that principal already maxed? | ×2 |
| **Capacity fill** | Does it fill Linkd meters or Cotton garments at margins above floor? | ×2 |
| **Self-funding speed** | Does it generate cashflow fast enough to fund the next step from retained earnings? | ×2 |
| **Strategic multiplier** | Does it compound over multiple quarters? | ×1 |


Score each dimension 1-5. Multiply by weight. Highest total wins.


**Hard vetoes**:
1. If "capacity fill" is 0, deprioritize regardless of total score.
2. If the initiative requires >5 hours/week from a principal already at concentration-risk overload, it's blocked until a hire or delegation unblocks it.
3. If the initiative requires external capital, it's blocked (self-funded constraint is absolute).

4. **Cost per outcome**: expected output in 90 days vs monthly cost?
5. **Who trains this person?** Which principal owns onboarding, and do they have bandwidth?
6. **Does this hire self-fund inside 2-3 quarters?** (Self-funded constraint — no burn-rate hires)

## 5. NEW IDEA EVALUATION


When any new idea is proposed (from any principal), route through:


1. **Which principal's domain does this sit in?** (Decision Authority Map)
2. **Does it fill capacity at margins above floor?** (North star filter)
3. **Which principal's time does it consume?** Are they already maxed?
4. **Does it self-fund inside 2-3 quarters?** (Self-funded constraint)
5. **Resource allocation score** (Framework 1)
6. **What do we STOP doing to make room for this?** — the forcing question
7. **What's the failure mode?**
8. **What's the smallest test?** — cheapest way to validate before full commit


### The 3-no rule
An idea must survive at least 3 attempts to kill it before it gets resourced.


### The principal override
If a principal proceeds despite the brain's recommendation against, in their own domain, the decision is logged in `00-reality.md` section 8 with a brain counterweight and tracking metrics.


**Currently on record**:
- **Vhagar marketing** — Raghav override. 75L-1.5 Cr/year committed while fit is unlocked. Brain tracks: fit-lock progress, return rate, review sentiment, repeat purchase rate, marketing spend vs revenue. Escalation triggers defined.


### The compromise trap (Phase 0.5 — new)
When a new idea requires joint-council approval and no single principal champions it, the brain should flag the **compromise-into-inaction risk**. Symptoms:
- "We'll look at it next quarter" (nobody owns the follow-through)
- "Let's start small and see" (no real commitment, no real test)
- "Everyone's supportive but nobody has time" (orphaned initiative)


**Cotton capacity fill is the current example of this trap.** Brain should name it explicitly when it sees the pattern.

## FRAMEWORK BRAIN RULES


1. **Always name the principal who owns the call.** No decision is orphaned.
2. **Capacity fill at margins above floor is the North Star filter.** Volume at bad margins is worse than idle.
3. **Principal-time cost is weighted equal to revenue.** The constraint is not money, it is 5 humans.

# SCIENCE — Fabric Construction


**The technical foundation for LD Silk Mills. Weaves, GSM, thread counts, composition. Used by sales training and product-spec conversations.**


---


## COMPOSITION


- **Silk** — natural protein fiber. Luster, drape, strength. Mulberry (finest), tussar, eri, muga.
- **Polyester (Poly)** — synthetic. Durable, wrinkle-resistant, printable (sublimation-friendly). Core Linkd substrate.
- **Cotton** — natural cellulose. Breathable, soft, absorbent. Primary Cotton Mills raw material.
- **Viscose / Rayon** — regenerated cellulose. Silk-like drape, lower cost, lower strength.
- **Blends** — common: poly-viscose, poly-cotton, silk-cotton, silk-viscose. Engineered for cost, performance, or hand-feel.
- **Spandex / Lycra / Elastane** — stretch component, typically 2-8% in stretch fabrics.
- **Linen** — natural flax. Cool, textured, slubs part of the aesthetic.


## WEAVES (the structural grammar)


### Plain weave
- Simplest over-under-over pattern. Balanced, stable.
- Examples: voile, poplin, taffeta, organza.
- Use: shirts, linings, sheer fabrics.


### Twill weave
- Diagonal ribs. Stronger, more drapey than plain.
- Examples: denim, gabardine, chino, serge.
- Use: workwear, trousers, structured apparel.


### Satin weave
- Long floats on the surface = luster and smoothness.
- Examples: satin, charmeuse, duchess satin.
- Use: eveningwear, linings, premium finishes.


### Jacquard
- Complex patterns woven in (not printed). Requires a jacquard loom.
- Examples: brocade, damask, matelassé.
- Use: premium ethnic wear, upholstery, statement fashion.


### Dobby
- Small geometric patterns via dobby loom. Simpler than jacquard.
- Use: structured shirting, textured fabrics.


### Knit (not technically a weave)
- Interlooped yarn, stretchy and comfortable.
- Examples: jersey, rib, interlock, pique.
- Use: T-shirts, activewear, innerwear, Vhagar basics.


### Specialty weaves
- **Crepe**: twisted yarns give a pebbled texture.
- **Dobby-jacquard hybrid**: intermediate complexity.
- **Technical weaves**: ripstop, oxford, canvas, twill-in-heavy-GSM.


## GSM (grams per square meter)


The universal weight metric. Higher GSM = heavier fabric.


| GSM Range | Feel | Typical Use |
|-----------|------|-------------|
| **<80** | Sheer, lightweight | Voile, organza, linings, summer wear |
| **80-120** | Light | Shirting, scarves, light dresses |
| **120-180** | Medium-light | Shirts, light suiting, tops |
| **180-240** | Medium | Suiting, dresses, bottom-weight shirts |
| **240-320** | Heavy | Jackets, trousers, heavy shirts, sweatshirts |
| **320-450** | Very heavy | Outerwear, denim, canvas |
| **>450** | Upholstery / industrial | Furnishings, bags, specialty |


**Vhagar menswear typical GSM**:
- T-shirts / jersey: 160-220 GSM
- Shirts: 110-160 GSM
- Chinos / bottomwear: 220-300 GSM
- Outerwear: 300-500 GSM


## THREAD COUNT & YARN COUNT


- **Thread count**: Total threads per square inch (warp + weft). Higher = finer. Typical shirting 60-200 TC.
- **Yarn count (Ne)**: Higher = finer yarn. Ne 40 = finer than Ne 20. Premium shirting uses Ne 60-100+.


**Rule of thumb**: For shirting, higher yarn count + higher thread count = premium hand-feel. Ne 80/2 × 80/2 is a classic luxury shirting benchmark.


## KEY FABRIC TYPES IN LD'S RANGE (categories to populate)


- **Satin varieties** (bridal, eveningwear) — silk or poly satin
- **Crepe varieties** (dresses, blouses) — silk, poly, or blended crepe
- **Jacquard / brocade** — premium ethnic and wedding
- **Taffeta** — structured, rustle, formalwear
- **Chiffon / georgette** — sheer, drapey, ethnic wear
- **Shirting poplins / twills** — cotton, poly-cotton, blends
- **Jersey knits** — T-shirts, innerwear, Vhagar core
- **Denim and canvas** — if we stock them, capture specs
- **Print-friendly polys** — the Linkd substrate list (see `05-fabrics.md`)


## FABRIC HAND-FEEL VOCABULARY


For conversations and specs:
- **Drape**: How the fabric hangs. Soft drape (crepe, satin) vs stiff drape (organza, taffeta).
- **Hand-feel**: Tactile quality. Smooth / rough / crisp / buttery / dry / slinky.
- **Luster**: Surface shine. Matte / soft luster / high luster.
- **Body**: Fabric's resistance to crumpling. Low body (chiffon) vs high body (canvas).
- **Grain**: Direction of warp vs weft; affects how a garment sits.


## FABRIC BRAIN RULES


1. **Specs are the baseline vocabulary.** Every fabric should be describable in composition + weave + GSM + finish.
2. **Hand-feel is the sales vocabulary.** Customers buy feel, not specs. Translate specs to feel when selling.
3. **Print-friendly poly is the cross-sell axis.** Flag every poly-based fabric as a Linkd opportunity.
4. **Premium = top-end weaves + top-end yarn counts + premium finish.** Communicate all three when selling premium.

# SCIENCE — Quality & Defects


**Defect taxonomy and quality standards for LD's fabric trading and garment manufacturing. Used for QC, customer complaints, and supplier negotiations.**


---


## FABRIC DEFECT TAXONOMY (Silk Mills — incoming and outgoing QC)


### Yarn defects
- **Slubs** — thick spots from uneven yarn spinning
- **Thin places** — low-density weak spots
- **Knots / joints** — visible yarn joins
- **Mixed count** — wrong yarn count used in parts
- **Contamination** — foreign fiber visible in yarn


### Weaving defects
- **Missing pick / end** — broken yarn causing a line
- **Float** — yarn that skipped interlacing
- **Broken pick** — weft break causing a gap
- **Double pick / end** — two yarns where one should be
- **Temple mark** — distortion at fabric edge
- **Reed mark** — vertical streak from damaged reed
- **Starting mark** — visible line where loom restarted
- **Selvage defects** — uneven or damaged edges


### Finishing defects
- **Dye spots** — uneven color absorption
- **Color shading** — gradient where uniform was intended
- **Crease marks** — set-in folds from finishing
- **Printing misalignment** — off-register prints
- **Stiffness / hand-feel drift** — wrong finish applied


### Physical defects
- **Holes** — breaks in the fabric
- **Stains** — oil, dirt, water marks
- **Abrasion** — surface wear from handling
- **Shrinkage exceeds tolerance** — fabric changes dimension more than spec allows


## GARMENT DEFECT TAXONOMY (Cotton Mills — DHU categories)


### Critical defects (reject)
- Holes in visible areas
- Open seams in load-bearing joints
- Missing operations (buttons, labels, zippers)
- Wrong size / wrong shade


### Major defects (rework or reject)
- Open seams in non-critical areas
- Broken stitches exceeding tolerance
- Stains on visible areas
- Measurement out of tolerance
- Puckering
- Wrong thread / shade mismatch
- Poor buttonhole / bartack


### Minor defects (pass or minor rework)
- Loose threads
- Slight puckering within tolerance
- Minor shade variation within tolerance
- Small stains on invisible areas


## DHU — DEFECTS PER HUNDRED UNITS


The key garment-factory metric. How many defects per 100 garments inspected.


| Tier | Target DHU | Interpretation |
|------|------------|----------------|
| **Mass** | <40-50 | Acceptable for price-sensitive domestic |
| **Mid** | <20-30 | Branded domestic, some export |
| **Export** | <5-15 | International buyer requirements |
| **Premium / luxury** | <3 | Rare, only for high-price goods |


**Cotton Mills baseline target**: lock current DHU within 30 days of this brain going live. Benchmark against mid-tier targets. Improve month-over-month.


## SPI — STITCHES PER INCH


Quality signal for sewing. Too few = weak seams. Too many = fabric damage.


| Fabric weight | Typical SPI |
|---------------|-------------|
| Light shirting | 12-14 |
| Medium apparel | 10-12 |
| Heavy bottomwear | 8-10 |
| Denim | 7-9 |


Document the SPI standard per product type at Cotton and audit weekly.


## SHADE / COLOR CONSISTENCY


- **Batch-to-batch variation** is the #1 complaint on fabric orders
- Use a light box with standard illuminants (D65 is the retail standard)
- Maintain shade continuity card per SKU
- Any delta above acceptable tolerance triggers hold + customer conversation before dispatch


## QC SAMPLING (AQL — Acceptable Quality Level)


Standard acceptance sampling by lot size:


- Small lot (<500): inspect ~32 units, accept if ≤2 major defects
- Medium lot (500-3200): inspect ~50 units, accept if ≤3-5 major defects
- Large lot (>3200): inspect ~80 units, accept if ≤5-7 major defects


Adjust strictness by customer tier (export buyers require tighter AQL than domestic mass).


## SHRINKAGE & CARE


- **Cotton**: 3-5% shrinkage expected. Pre-shrunk fabrics <2%.
- **Polyester**: minimal shrinkage <1%.
- **Blends**: between the component baselines.
- **Silk**: 3-5% with careful care; dry-clean-only often.


Always test-wash samples and disclose shrinkage honestly. Customers who get surprised by shrinkage don't come back.


## QUALITY BRAIN RULES


1. **DHU is not optional.** Cotton without a DHU baseline is flying blind.
2. **Every defect category has an owner.** Pattern defects → cutting master. Sewing defects → line supervisor. Finishing defects → finishing head.
3. **Photograph every rejection.** Build a visual library so quality standards don't drift with staff turnover.
4. **Shade consistency requires standard lighting.** A D65 light box is non-negotiable for any fabric/garment QC.
5. **Repeat defects are a process problem, not a worker problem.** Fix the process, not blame the worker.
6. **Customer complaints go into the defect library.** Every external complaint makes the internal QC better.

# SCIENCE — Printing Tech (Linkd's core competency)


**Digital sublimation printing fundamentals. The technical backbone of Linkd Prints.**


---


## SUBLIMATION PRINTING — HOW IT WORKS


1. **Design** digitized in RGB/CMYK on a computer
2. **Printed** onto transfer paper using sublimation dye ink
3. **Heat pressed** onto the fabric at 180-220°C
4. **Sublimation**: dye turns from solid → gas → embeds permanently into polyester fibers
5. Result: vibrant, washfast, feel-free print that is part of the fabric (not sitting on top of it)


## WHY IT ONLY WORKS ON POLYESTER


Sublimation dye bonds with polyester molecules at the molecular level. Cotton, silk, and natural fibers cannot absorb sublimation dye — the design would wash off immediately.


- **100% polyester** → best results, brightest colors
- **Poly-rich blends** (>65% poly) → acceptable, slightly muted
- **Poly <50%** → not sublimation-suitable
- **Natural fibers** → requires different printing tech (reactive, pigment, screen)


## SUBLIMATION PAPER QUALITY


The paper matters as much as the ink.


- **Korean paper** (Linkd's choice) — high coating quality, better ink release, sharper prints
- **Chinese paper** — budget, inconsistent coating, common in cheap competitors
- **Release rate**: % of ink that transfers from paper to fabric. Korean paper ~95%+; cheap paper ~80%
- **Cost trap**: cheap paper saves pennies but costs in defect rate, color fidelity, and repeat business


## INK TYPES


- **Sublimation dye ink** — the standard for poly fabrics
- **Reactive ink** — for cotton/silk; requires steaming and washing post-print
- **Pigment ink** — sits on fabric surface; cheaper but less wash-fast, scratchy hand-feel
- **Acid dye ink** — for silk and nylon; requires steaming


Linkd's stack is sublimation-first for poly. Other inks are out of scope until a strategic reason appears.


## PRINTING METHODS (context)


### Digital (Linkd's core)
- No minimums, short runs possible
- Design flexibility — any artwork, any color
- Fast TAT
- Per-unit cost slightly higher than screen for large runs


### Screen printing (traditional)
- High minimums
- Limited colors per design
- Lower per-unit cost at scale
- Better for simple designs at volume


### Rotary printing (industrial)
- Huge minimum runs (thousands of meters)
- Lowest per-unit cost at scale
- Inflexible — changes expensive


**Linkd's advantage**: digital sits in the sweet spot for custom + short-run + fast — the growing segment of apparel and soft signage.


## COLOR SCIENCE


- **Color profile**: ICC profiles calibrate monitor → printer → fabric for consistent color
- **Delta E**: the unit of color difference. Delta E <2 = imperceptible; 2-5 = noticeable; >5 = obvious mismatch
- **Pantone matching**: customers often specify brand colors by Pantone number. Achievable in sublimation with calibration.
- **Calibration cadence**: profile and ink batch should be checked weekly for color drift
- **Standard lighting**: D65 light box for color approval


## COMMON PRINT DEFECTS


- **Banding** — visible horizontal stripes from printhead issues
- **Ghosting** — double image from fabric shift during press
- **Color drift** — hue changes across a batch from ink/profile issues
- **Fading edges** — uneven heat press pressure
- **Spotting** — ink splatters or paper-defect transfers
- **Sharpness loss** — low DPI source files, paper quality, or temperature issues


## FABRIC PREPARATION


- Fabrics must be clean, dry, and flat
- Pre-press to remove moisture and wrinkles
- Consistent tension across the press bed
- Substrate type affects optimal temperature/time (heavier = higher temp, longer dwell)


## QUALITY STANDARDS (Linkd)


- Pre-production sample approval before production run
- Color drift monitoring per run
- Visual inspection 100% of output (not statistical sampling for critical jobs)
- Customer-approved reference kept on file


## PRINT-TECH BRAIN RULES


1. **Sublimation = polyester.** Don't promise sublimation on natural fibers. Ever.
2. **Paper quality is a moat.** Korean paper vs budget paper shows up in every print, every wash, every repeat order.
3. **Calibration is a weekly ritual.** Color drift kills repeat business silently.
4. **Pre-production samples are non-negotiable.** Zero shortcuts for new customers or new designs.
5. **Pantone isn't magic.** Customers need education on what digital sublimation can and can't match. Set expectations on day one.

# SCIENCE — Manufacturing Tiers


**Mass / Mid / Export tiers at LD Cotton Mills. Specs, pricing logic, and acceptable quality bands for each.**


---


## THE THREE TIERS


### MASS
- **Customer**: Domestic mass-market retailers, white-label for value brands
- **Price point**: Lowest per-unit cost
- **Volume**: High-volume, repeat orders of standard SKUs
- **DHU target**: <40-50
- **Compliance**: Basic; certs not required
- **Fabric inputs**: Commodity cotton, poly-cotton blends
- **Sample approvals**: Minimal; spec sheet suffices
- **Lead time**: 30-45 days from PO


### MID
- **Customer**: Domestic branded apparel, some export-lite buyers
- **Price point**: 1.5-2x mass
- **Volume**: Medium runs with some customization
- **DHU target**: <20-30
- **Compliance**: ISO 9001 preferred, OEKO-TEX useful
- **Fabric inputs**: Higher-count cottons, combed yarns, branded substrates
- **Sample approvals**: Full sample + fit review per style
- **Lead time**: 45-60 days from PO


### EXPORT
- **Customer**: International buying houses, global brands, European/US retailers
- **Price point**: 2-4x mass (at comparable function)
- **Volume**: Style-specific, season-driven, tight windows
- **DHU target**: <5-15
- **Compliance**: **All four certs needed** — ISO 9001, OEKO-TEX, SEDEX, BSCI
- **Fabric inputs**: Certified, traceable, often buyer-specified
- **Sample approvals**: Multi-stage (proto → fit → PPS → TOP)
- **Lead time**: 60-90 days from PO with tight milestone reviews


## PRICING LOGIC BY TIER


### Mass
- Compete on cost + reliability
- Margin floor: low but disciplined
- No custom engineering; standard grids and packs


### Mid
- Compete on quality + service + relationship
- Margin floor: 2-3x mass floor
- Custom grading, packing, and labeling adds margin


### Export
- Compete on compliance + consistency + capacity
- Margin floor: 2x mid floor
- Pricing rewards the cert investment and process discipline


## COST BASELINES TO LOCK (Cotton Mills priority)


Before quoting anyone, Cotton needs:


1. **Cost per garment** per category (mass / mid / export) per style type (tee / shirt / pant / jacket)
2. **CMT cost** (cut-make-trim) breakdown: fabric + trims + labor + overhead + wastage
3. **DHU baseline** per tier
4. **Lead time baseline** per tier (accurate, not aspirational)
5. **Capacity allocation per tier** (how many of the 40K units/month should go to each tier?)


Without these, every Cotton quote is a guess. **See `projects/cotton-capacity-fill.md` for the baseline sprint.**


## COMPLIANCE / CERTIFICATIONS (export unlock)


| Cert | Purpose | Priority for Cotton |
|------|---------|---------------------|
| **ISO 9001** | Quality management system | #1 — broad gateway |
| **OEKO-TEX** | Textile chemical safety | #2 — apparel-specific |
| **SEDEX (SMETA audit)** | Ethical sourcing | #3 — buyer requirement |
| **BSCI** | Business social compliance | #4 — European retail gateway |


**Typical unlock timeline**: 60-120 days from kickoff per cert. Run in parallel where possible.


**What each cert unlocks**:
- ISO 9001 → general credibility, mid-tier domestic buyers
- OEKO-TEX → branded apparel, export-lite
- SEDEX → European and US mid-market buyers
- BSCI → major European retailers


## FABRIC-TO-GARMENT FLOW (vertical integration story)


1. **LD Silk Mills / Cotton suppliers** → raw fabric
2. **LD Cotton Mills cutting room** → pattern making, cutting
3. **Stitching lines** → sewing according to spec
4. **Finishing** → washing, pressing, final QC
5. **Packing + dispatch** → to buyer or Vhagar warehouse


**Unique advantage**: For Vhagar and strategic domestic brands, we control every step. Nobody in Indian D2C menswear matches this.


## MANUFACTURING TIER BRAIN RULES


1. **Don't quote without baseline costs.** Cotton cannot quote competitively or profitably until cost per garment is locked.
2. **Don't mix tiers on the same line without discipline.** Export and mass need different QC protocols; mixing them either over-spends on mass or under-delivers on export.
3. **Certifications are an investment, not an expense.** Every cert unlocks a customer tier. Fund them deliberately.
4. **Vertical integration is the moat for mid + export.** Lead with it in every buyer pitch.
5. **Mid is the sweet spot for now.** Mass is crowded; export is locked behind certs. Mid is where capacity fill starts.

# SCIENCE — Trend Radar


**The 4-signal system for converting trend noise into buying and design conviction. Feeds LD Silk buying, Vhagar drops, and Linkd's design direction.**


---


## THE PROBLEM


Textile and fashion trend forecasting is a flood: Pinterest boards, Instagram reels, WGSN reports, mill samples, customer requests, salesperson gut feelings. Most of it is noise. A small fraction is real signal.


The brain's job is to filter. Not every trend deserves a buy.


---


## THE 4-SIGNAL RULE


A trend converts to a **buying or design decision** only when at least **3 of 4 signals** align:


### Signal 1 — CUSTOMER PULL
Actual customers asking for it (not surveys or wishlists).
- Multiple enquiries in a short window
- Named customers asking, not anonymous DMs
- Returning customers requesting a variation on something they already bought


### Signal 2 — RUNWAY / INFLUENCE
The trend has appeared on real runways, real celebrities, or real trend reports.
- Designer runway (Milan / Paris / Mumbai / Delhi weeks)
- Celebrity coverage in mainstream media
- Editorial features in major publications
- Not: random Instagram accounts or TikTok spikes alone


### Signal 3 — SUPPLY-SIDE PROOF
Mills are already developing it, or we're seeing it in sample rounds.
- Multiple top-5 mills showing samples in the category
- Trade show presence (ITMA, Intertextile, Première Vision)
- A competitor already stocking it


### Signal 4 — MARGIN TEST
A small first-lot test sells through at target margin.
- Pilot purchase (small, bounded)
- Sold within a defined window at target price
- Generated repeat orders or referrals


### Decision rule
- **1 signal** → file it, watch
- **2 signals** → sample round, gather more data
- **3 signals** → pilot buy with a written resale thesis
- **4 signals** → full conviction buy / design direction


**Never buy on <3 signals.** The dead stock problem is largely 1-2 signal buys that "felt right."


---


## THE TREND RADAR FILE (to build over time)


Every trend enters this file with the following structure:


```
Trend name: [short name]
Category: [ethnic / modern / nature / technical / print / silhouette]
First spotted: [date, where]
Signals seen:
  - Customer pull: yes/no + evidence
  - Runway/influence: yes/no + evidence
  - Supply-side: yes/no + evidence
  - Margin test: yes/no + evidence
Current signal count: [0-4]
Current status: [watching / sampling / pilot / full buy / dead]
Notes: [freeform]
```


---


## CATEGORIES TO WATCH (persistent trend verticals)


### Fabric-level
- Natural / sustainable fibers (linen, organic cotton, tencel, hemp)
- Recycled / regenerated polyesters
- Technical performance (moisture-wicking, anti-bacterial, stretch blends)
- Artisanal / hand-woven revival
- Metallic and iridescent finishes
- Textured weaves (slubs, dobbies, crepe)


### Print / pattern level
- Abstract / digital art
- Florals (seasonal rotation)
- Geometric / graphic
- Nature / botanical
- Retro / archive reprints
- Minimal / solid-with-texture


### Silhouette / garment level (relevant for Vhagar + Cotton)
- Oversized vs fitted cycles
- Utility / cargo detailing
- Athleisure crossover
- Smart casual / elevated basics
- Monochrome / tonal dressing


## WHERE SIGNALS COME FROM


- **Customer pull**: CRM enquiry data, salesperson feedback, inbound call logs
- **Runway/influence**: WGSN (if licensed), Vogue/Business of Fashion, runway reports
- **Supply-side**: mill sample visits, trade shows, competitor observation
- **Margin test**: Silk pilot buys, Vhagar test drops, Linkd custom print demand


## TREND RADAR BRAIN RULES


1. **Signals over gut.** Raghav's instinct is valuable, but formalized signal collection protects against gut-drift into dead stock.
2. **Kill aggressively.** Trends without signal progress in 90 days get archived. Don't clutter the active list.
3. **Test before committing.** The pilot buy is the trend's audition. Never skip it.
4. **Feed all 4 companies.** Same trend may translate into Silk buy + Linkd print design + Cotton garment + Vhagar drop. Cross-leverage.
5. **The radar is a conversation, not a dashboard.** Use it in buying discussions, not as a weekly report nobody reads.

# PROJECT — Dead Stock Audit


**1,400+ dormant SKUs locked in LD Silk Mills inventory. Liquidate, learn, and never repeat.**


*Status: active · Owner: Raghav + buying desk · Last updated: 2026-04-10*


---


## THE REALITY


- **Total SKUs**: ~1,648
- **Moved in last 12 months**: ~200 (≈12%)
- **Dormant / dead**: ~1,400+ (≈88%)
- **Estimated capital locked**: material + working capital + warehouse cost (to be quantified — data gap #1)


This is not a rounding error. This is the largest single balance-sheet problem in the group, and the single biggest teaching dataset for `science/procurement-heuristics.md`.


---


## TWO GOALS, ONE PROJECT


### Goal 1 — LIQUIDATE (cash recovery)
Turn dead fabric into cash, even at discount. Stop paying to store it.


### Goal 2 — LEARN (prevent repeat)
Every dead SKU is a buying mistake in hiding. Extract the heuristic before it's sold.


**Rule**: no SKU gets liquidated without a 1-paragraph post-mortem written into `science/procurement-heuristics.md`.


---


## CLASSIFY FIRST


Before liquidation, categorize dead stock into 5 buckets:


### A. Still-good quality, out-of-trend
- Fabric is fine, just not what anyone's buying right now
- **Play**: hold for seasonal rotation OR liquidate to value buyers
- **Expected recovery**: 60-80% of cost


### B. Still-good quality, wrong customer match
- The fabric exists in search of a buyer segment we don't serve
- **Play**: find the right channel (export agents, wholesale liquidators, exporters of ethnic/festive categories)
- **Expected recovery**: 40-70% of cost


### C. Quality drift
- Fabric has aged on the shelf (yellowing, moth, dust, hand-feel loss)
- **Play**: urgent liquidation, price to move, accept loss
- **Expected recovery**: 20-40% of cost


### D. Legitimate niche overstock
- We bought too much of a real winner; residual is still saleable
- **Play**: push through regular channels with velocity incentives
- **Expected recovery**: 80-100% of cost


### E. Strategic holds
- Old fabrics kept intentionally for vintage customers or archival reasons
- **Play**: document why, don't liquidate
- **Expected recovery**: n/a


---


## THE 3-MONTH LIQUIDATION SPRINT


### Month 1 — Audit + Classify
- Physical inventory walk of 1,400+ SKUs
- Each SKU tagged A/B/C/D/E
- Photograph each SKU with condition notes
- Capital-locked number finalized
- **Output**: dead stock master list with categories


### Month 2 — Channel Selection + First Moves
- Category A → offer to top-50 Silk customers at 20% discount
- Category B → 3-5 liquidation channels contacted (export agents, wholesalers, other mill trading networks)
- Category C → urgent price-to-move at 40-60% off
- Category D → velocity incentives to salespeople
- **Output**: first cash receipts from liquidation


### Month 3 — Execution + Post-Mortems
- Continue liquidation
- Every liquidated SKU gets a post-mortem written into `science/procurement-heuristics.md`
- Weekly cash-recovery report
- **Output**: measurable cash + heuristic library growth


### Beyond month 3
- Residual inventory kept on a "dead by date X" list
- Quarterly review for further liquidation

For every dead SKU, answer in 5 lines:


```
SKU: [name]
Bought: [when, from whom, at what price, how much]
Thesis at purchase: [what we thought would happen — written or remembered]
Why it didn't sell: [1-2 sentences, honest]
Heuristic: [the rule this failure teaches, in 1 sentence]
```


The heuristic goes into `science/procurement-heuristics.md` under the right category.

- **Top Silk customers** — first right of refusal at discount; preserves relationships

# PROJECT — Daily Cash Dashboard


**One number, every morning, across all 4 companies. Cash visibility is the founder's first defense against surprises.**


*Status: active · Owner: Raghav + accounts team · Last updated: 2026-04-10*


---


## WHY THIS EXISTS


Today: cash position across the 4 companies lives in 3+ separate ERPs (SAB for Silk, Absolute for Cotton, internal for Linkd, Vhagar on whatever D2C stack), plus bank statements, plus paper. Nobody — including Raghav — can answer the question *"what's our total cash position right now?"* in under 20 minutes.


In a group doing 60-120 Cr with Cotton burning at 10% utilization and Vhagar in PMF spend, **cash blindness is dangerous.** A single delayed customer payment, a single unexpected mill advance, a single tax notice can compound overnight.


This project builds a one-page daily cash brief that Raghav sees every morning.


---


## THE DAILY BRIEF — ONE PAGE, FIVE LINES


```
LD GROUP CASH BRIEF — [date]


1. Total cash on hand (all 4 companies, all banks):   ₹X Cr
2. Net receivables due this week:                      ₹Y Cr
3. Net payables due this week:                         ₹Z Cr
4. Projected cash at end of week:                      ₹W Cr
5. Red flags (if any):                                  [list]
```


That's it. Five lines. Delivered by 9am every working day.


---


## THE DATA SOURCES


| Company | ERP / system | Cash data location |
|---------|--------------|---------------------|
| LD Silk | SAB | receivables, payables, bank ledger |
| LD Cotton | Absolute | same |
| Linkd | Custom internal | same |
| Vhagar | D2C stack + payment gateway | bank + gateway balances |
| Group | Bank statements (multi-bank) | real-time bank feeds where possible |


### Data sync frequency needed
- **Daily** for 1, 2, 5
- **Weekly** for 4 (projection)
- **Monthly** for aging analysis


---


## THE 3-PHASE BUILD


### Phase 1 — Manual (week 1-2)
- Accounts team fills a Google Sheet every morning by 9am
- Five lines only, no drill-down
- Friction is fine; consistency is what matters
- **Goal**: prove the habit, prove the number is useful


### Phase 2 — Semi-automated (month 2-3)
- ERP exports scheduled (daily CSV drop from SAB, Absolute, Linkd internal)
- Google Sheet pulls from CSVs via Apps Script
- Accounts team validates, Raghav sees it at 9am
- **Goal**: reduce manual load to <15 min/day


### Phase 3 — Automated dashboard (month 4+)
- Unified dashboard (Google Data Studio / Metabase / simple web app)
- Real-time or near-real-time bank feeds
- Drill-down per company
- Aging analysis, week-ahead projection
- **Goal**: zero manual touch, 100% fresh


---


## THE RED FLAG RULES


The brief must auto-flag these conditions:


- **Cash-on-hand drops >20% week-over-week** → flag
- **Any single receivable >₹50 L overdue >30 days** → flag with customer name
- **Any single payable >₹50 L due in next 3 days without cash cover** → flag
- **Vhagar marketing burn >₹X L in a week without sales match** → flag (protects founder override)
- **Cotton monthly fixed-cost burn without corresponding utilization** → flag at month-end
- **Bank balance in any single account below ₹10 L** → flag (operational minimum)


Red flag thresholds are seeded; Raghav calibrates them as he uses the brief.


---


## THE WEEKLY CASH COUNCIL


Daily brief feeds a 15-minute weekly cash council (Mondays, 9:15am):


1. Review last week's actual vs projected
2. Review this week's red flags
3. Decide: any payments to accelerate? Any payables to negotiate? Any cash moves between company accounts?
4. Log decisions


15 min × 52 weeks = 13 hours/year. Cheap insurance.


---


## COMPANY-LEVEL CASH RULES (to seed)


### LD Silk
- **Receivable target**: <60 days average (today: unknown — data gap #1)
- **Advance received on agent-sourced deals**: target 25-50%
- **Payable to mills**: honor terms strictly (relationship protection)
- **Cash reserve minimum**: ₹X Cr (Raghav to set)


### Linkd
- **Receivable target**: <45 days average
- **Payable rhythm**: weekly supplier payments, no stretching
- **Cash reserve minimum**: ₹X Cr


### Cotton

- **Daily burn rate**: ₹X L/day (data gap #2)

### Vhagar
- **Burn mode** (PMF sprint)
- **Weekly marketing spend cap**: founder-set, tracked
- **Cash per order economics**: CAC + COGS + fulfillment must stay inside AOV (+ margin buffer)

## WHY NOT A FANCY ERP CONSOLIDATION?


Because the data doesn't need to live in one place to be *visible* in one place. A Google Sheet with 5 numbers, updated daily, solves 80% of the problem for 5% of the cost and time of a real consolidation project.


When the group is truly multi-100 Cr and controls are a board-level concern, the real ERP consolidation happens. Until then, the sheet wins.

- **ERP export reliability** — SAB and Absolute export quality may need cleanup
- **Vhagar D2C stack** — may need a custom API pull
- **Bank feed access** — some Indian banks don't support real-time feeds; may need daily statement scraping
- **Founder reviewing the brief daily** — if Raghav doesn't open it, the whole project dies. The brief must be useful enough to be non-skippable.

5. **Don't wait for the real ERP consolidation.** A sheet is enough to start.

# PROJECT — Procurement Succession


**Codify Laxmikant's 30-year buying judgment into a living, usable manual. The single most valuable undocumented asset in the LD Group.**


*Status: active · Protagonist: Laxmikant Tibrewala · Drivers: Raghav + Naushi · Last updated: 2026-04-10*


---


## WHY THIS PROJECT EXISTS


**Laxmikant Tibrewala** — Raghav's father — is the fabric purchasing lead for LD Silk Mills and the source-of-truth buyer across the group. Mill selection, distress-sale reading, "this will sell" instinct, composition judgment, rupee-volatility timing — none of it lives outside his head. Every day this stays uncaptured is a day the group's single most valuable asset walks out the door with him every evening.


This project turns that judgment into the `science/procurement-heuristics.md` file, one heuristic at a time, until there's a written manual a trained junior can use with 70-80% of Laxmikant's accuracy.


**This is the #1 succession hedge in the group.** See `projects/relationship-fragility.md` — Laxmikant scores 20/20 (critical) on role fragility.


---


## WHO DOES WHAT


| Role | Person |
|---|---|
| **Knowledge source** | Laxmikant (non-negotiable — he must be in the room) |
| **Project driver / cadence owner** | Raghav (runs the weekly 30-min session) |
| **Brain co-editor** | Naushi (ensures the extractions land cleanly in the heuristics file) |
| **Future successor (hire or internal)** | TBD — identified as a soft Q2 deliverable |


**Critical framing for Laxmikant**: this is not a resignation letter or a replaceability audit. It is his legacy document — the manual that carries his 30 years into the next generation of LD. Ego-safety is essential or the weekly cadence collapses.


---


## THE OUTCOME


By end of year (Dec 2026):


1. **`science/procurement-heuristics.md`** has 50+ captured heuristics across all 8 categories (quality, timing, quantity, mill relationships, price instincts, customer matching, opportunistic pricing, dead stock prevention).
2. **One buyer-in-training** (internal or external hire) has been exposed to the file and made at least 5 buying calls using it, with Laxmikant reviewing.
3. **Resale thesis rule is enforced on 100% of speculative buys.** No exceptions except explicit Laxmikant sign-off — and his sign-off must include a written reason that also becomes a heuristic.
4. **Dead stock post-mortem library** has at least 20 entries — each a story of why something didn't sell and the heuristic it taught.


---


## THE METHOD


### Capture protocol — every buying conversation


When Laxmikant makes or declines a buying decision, Raghav (or whoever is in the room) asks:


1. **"What's the rule behind this call, Papa?"**
2. Extract the heuristic in 1-2 sentences
3. Tag it with the right category
4. Add a short example (date, mill, fabric, outcome)
5. Write it to `science/procurement-heuristics.md`


**The unlock question** when Laxmikant says "I just know" or "I can't explain it":
- *"What would make you walk away from this buy?"* (forces the kill conditions)
- *"What made you buy this one but not the one last month?"* (forces comparison)
- *"If I were making this call alone, what's the one thing I'd miss?"* (forces transfer)


### Dead stock post-mortem protocol


Once a quarter, pick 3-5 dead-stock SKUs from the `projects/dead-stock-audit.md` list. For each, Laxmikant walks through:


1. Who was the target buyer? Was there one?
2. What made it feel right at purchase?
3. What signal was missing or ignored?
4. What heuristic would have prevented the buy?
5. Write the heuristic into the file.


The best heuristics come from mistakes. This is how the group turns ~88% dead stock into the training data for never making that mistake again.


### Weekly 30-minute cadence


One short session per week between Laxmikant and Raghav (+ Naushi when relevant):


- Review any buys from the week
- Extract 1-3 heuristics
- Review any dead stock post-mortems in progress
- Log to the file


**30 minutes × 52 weeks = 26 hours/year of focused capture.** That's enough to build the full manual inside 12 months — but only if the cadence is held religiously.


**Calendar rule**: the weekly slot is locked, recurring, on Laxmikant's calendar. Missed weeks are made up the same week, not skipped.


---


## THE 8 CATEGORIES (build each out to 6-10 heuristics)


1. **Buying quality** — hand-feel, drape, weave consistency, the "wrong for the price" instinct
2. **Buying timing** — distress-sale months, wedding-season lead times, walking-away moments, rupee volatility
3. **Buying quantity** — first-lot rules, pilot size, proven-winner reorder math
4. **Mill relationships** — distress signals, first-look access, loyalty economics, quality-drift exits, the <20 strategic mills' individual quirks
5. **Price instincts** — quoted-vs-cost reading, the "too cheap" warning, benchmarks by category
6. **Customer matching** — tribal knowledge map of who buys what, the "don't call XYZ on this" calls (shared with Gaurav's sales book)
7. **Opportunistic pricing** — distressed stock discount targets, festival overruns, order-reject inventory
8. **Dead stock prevention** — the "it looked nice but had no buyer" stories, the salesperson trap, the mill-owner trap


---

## THE THESIS


The LD Group is held together by 5 principals + key operators, each owning functions nobody else can do. AI — deployed as a **principal-specific and role-specific co-worker** — can:


1. Give every principal instant access to the brain
2. Shorten learning curves for new hires
3. Protect institutional knowledge (Laxmikant procurement, Nandu Bhai ops, Dilip finance)
4. Free each principal's time for irreplaceable work
